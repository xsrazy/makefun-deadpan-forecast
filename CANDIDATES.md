# Candidate selection

Ten candidate lines were written for the bounty "Deadpan Forecasts for
Cities That Do Not Exist" and judged head-to-head against the judge's
criteria, in order: (1) a one-line weather forecast for a city that
does not exist, absurd yet believable; (2) funnier, with a deadpan
twist that lands on a first read; (3) more vivid, with a concrete
image, number or detail; (4) reads like a real broadcast line; (5)
uses its 160 characters well, tight and with no padding.

## The candidates

1. (116 chars) In Brindlewick, 61 and overcast, with light rain expected at 3, the kind that soaks only the left side of your coat.
2. (101 chars) In Grimbleton, highs of 58, lows of 57, and fog so thick the lighthouse has filed a formal complaint.
3. (93 chars) Murkford wakes to 44 and drizzle, with a 70% chance the sun shows up late and blames traffic.
4. (95 chars) Thistledown: 72 and sunny, except for one cloud that has followed Mrs. Abernathy since Tuesday.
5. (106 chars) Vexley: 39 with sideways sleet and winds of 22 miles an hour, gusting to whatever the weathervane decides.
6. (113 chars) In Nether Poppleton, 66 and humid, with thunderstorms at 5 that the mayor has asked residents to take personally.
7. (98 chars) Drizzlewick: 51, rain all day, with a brief sunny spell at noon that nobody will believe happened.
8. (104 chars) Quilliam: 84 and clear, with a 10% chance of hail the size of golf balls and regrets the size of houses.
9. (106 chars) Foggarty: 48 and misty, visibility down to one lamppost, and the fog is not expected to lift before lunch.
10. (94 chars) In Wexford-under-Water, 59 and damp, with tides due at noon and the ducks already in position.

All ten pass criterion 1 (fictional city, one-line forecast) and the
160-character gate, so the decision was made on criteria 2-5.

## Head-to-head

- Round 1: #2 beat #1 (two jokes vs one gentle one), #4 beat #3 (a
  named resident stalked by one cloud is a sharper, more specific
  image than the sun blaming traffic), #6 beat #5 (bureaucratic
  deadpan beats a slightly random weathervane gag), #8 beat #7 (the
  hail/regrets escalation is a bigger laugh than the unbelievable
  sunny spell), #9 beat #10 (visibility down to one lamppost is a
  more vivid concrete image than ducks in position).
- Round 2: #2 beat #4 in the closest call. #4 is the most original
  single image, but #2 packs two jokes (the 58/57 high/low and the
  lighthouse complaint) and opens with textbook forecast cadence, so
  it wins on funniness and on broadcast tone. #6 beat #8: the mayor
  asking residents to take thunderstorms personally is cleaner
  deadpan than the hail/regrets line, whose second half drifts from
  what a forecaster would say.
- Final: #2 beat #6. The lighthouse filing a formal complaint lands
  faster than the mayor's request, and the near-identical high/low
  gives #2 a second laugh #6 does not have. #2 is also the most
  broadcast-plausible line of the ten: highs, lows, fog, and a
  complaint filed through proper channels.

## Winner

**#2 (101 characters):**

> In Grimbleton, highs of 58, lows of 57, and fog so thick the lighthouse has filed a formal complaint.

Runner-up: #4 (Thistledown, the cloud following Mrs. Abernathy).
The final line is the only line written to `entry.txt`; no other
candidate appears in `main.py`.
